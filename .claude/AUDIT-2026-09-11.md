A fresh review of the whole tree, not a diff. Nothing was grandfathered: a rationale
comment was treated as a claim to verify, not as a closed question, and the exported API
was open to challenge. Six reviewers read their scopes whole; the main session verified

**This file is a dated snapshot, superseded wholesale by the next audit.** It is not

## A. Exploitable — FIXED 2026-09-12

### A1. A flood of junk ends the collection window — discovery returns nothing
`wsd/discover.go:474` · *untrusted-input; reproduced by the main session*

The loop condition `len(result) < maxReplies && retained < maxReplyBytes` stops
**collecting**, not merely retaining. Every datagram counts, including non-XML with no
`RelatesTo`.

Measured on a real loopback socket: a flood of 7-byte `<junk/>` closed a **3-second window
after 2.99 ms** with 512 junk replies and `err == nil`. A conformant Target Service picks a
random delay of up to `appMaxDelay` (1 s) before answering a multicast Probe — by design,
to avoid a reply storm — so it is *never heard*. `Discover` returns zero devices and no
error, indistinguishable from an empty link.

No forgery, no message ID, no source spoofing. Any host on the link silences discovery.

The comment at `wsd/discover.go:463-473` argues that reading on "would leave the caller paying for the
flood in wakeups and copies without gaining a reply". That reasoning holds only if the
flood is the sole traffic; it overlooks that the protocol *mandates* honest replies arrive
later than the flood does.

**Fixed** in two parts, because the obvious half is hollow on its own.

1. *The window belongs to the clock.* `readReplies` now reads until its deadline and stops
   only appending past the caps. Pinned by `TestAFloodDoesNotCloseTheCollectionWindow`,
   which floods with **correlated** datagrams — the case only the loop condition handles.
2. *Noise gets its own allowance.* Measured after part 1 alone: the flood simply filled
   `maxReplies` instead, the late answer was read and dropped, and discovery still returned
   nothing. `readReplies` now takes the Probe's identifier and retains datagrams that do not
   carry it against a separate `maxUnknownReplies = 64`. Pinned end to end by
   `TestNoiseCannotTakeTheRoomAnAnswerNeeds`.

Each half was verified to fail its own test when reverted alone.

**What this does not close.** A host on the link receives our Probe — it is multicast — so
it can echo the identifier and draw on `maxReplies`. No retention policy can tell that
host's answers from a device's. The cost of silencing discovery rises from "send bytes" to
"read our Probe and echo it"; it does not become impossible. What the caps can no longer
decide is when the window ends.

**Cost.** Under a flood the loop now reads and drops for the rest of the window: one syscall
and one copy per datagram. The datagrams arrive whether or not the loop reads them, the
sender pays the same to produce them, and the window is still bounded by `MaxProbeTimeout`.

**Side effect on `SendProbe`.** It hands back raw datagrams, and now retains at most 64 that
do not correlate with its own Probe. Strictly more useful, but it is a behaviour change.

### A2. First-wins dedup lets a forger take a real camera's identity
`wsd/discover.go:234,273` · *untrusted-input*

`dedupKey` is `"uuid:"+UUID` and `seen[key]` keeps the **first** reply in arrival order. A
device's endpoint reference is multicast in every Hello and every ProbeMatches on the link,
so it is not a secret. A host that answers our Probe immediately with a camera's UUID and
its own `XAddrs` wins, because the camera's genuine reply is delayed up to `appMaxDelay`
and is then discarded as a duplicate.

The same `appMaxDelay` that makes A1 work makes A2 reliable: the honest party is required
to be slow.

The caller then dials the forger — and with `gosoap`'s UsernameToken, hands it a digest and
nonce over that connection.

**Fixed:** `dedupKey` now pairs the endpoint reference with the address it advertises.

Keying on the observed source instead was considered and rejected: a source address is
spoofable over UDP, and a device answering on both IP families reports two of them for one
machine. The advertised address is what the caller dials, so it is what must separate two
answers.

**Behaviour change, and it is visible.** A device that advertises a *different* address per
IP family is now reported once per address instead of once. Two existing tests asserted the
old collapse — `TestDedupKeyPrefersEndpointReference` said in as many words that "the same
endpoint reference must dedupe across addresses" — and both were rewritten to state the
reversal and why. This function cannot tell a dual-stack device from two hosts claiming one
identity; reporting both is the honest result, and a caller that wants one entry per
endpoint reference can group by UUID, which it could not do when the conflict was hidden.

Pinned by `TestAForgerDoesNotDisplaceADeviceItImpersonates`,
`TestDedupKeyPairsTheEndpointReferenceWithItsAddress` and
`TestDiscoverDeduplicatesAcrossPayloads`; all three fail if the key is reverted.

A3 below is still open and is what makes this visible to an operator.

### A3. `Device.From` never reaches the operator on the probe path — STILL OPEN
`bin/wsdc/discover.go:184` · *untrusted-input*

`printDevice` prints interface, UUID, URL. `Device.From` — the one field observed rather
than claimed, kept end-to-end for exactly this purpose per `wsd/device.go:35-43` — is
dropped, while `printAnnouncement` (`bin/wsdc/listen.go:174`) does print it. An operator

## B. Defects

### B1. The cancellation watchdog has no test at all
`wsd/discover.go:449-457`, `wsd/discover_test.go:259` · *runtime-safety; reproduced*

`TestReadRepliesHonoursCancelledContext` pins nothing. `fakeConn.ReadFrom` returns
`os.ErrDeadlineExceeded` on the first call whenever `replies` is empty, so `readReplies`
returns in microseconds with or without the watchdog.

Deleting the entire `stop`/`go func` block leaves **the whole `wsd` package green**,
including the test named for the mechanism. `.github/workflows/ci.yml` justifies `-race` on the grounds that
"both read paths unblock a blocked reader by moving its socket deadline from another
goroutine" — and that is the mechanism with zero coverage.

A test needs a conn that actually blocks until its deadline passes; `fakeConn` records
deadlines and honours none.

### B2. `probe` discards replies it already collected
`wsd/discover.go:370` · *go-architect and runtime-safety, independently*

`payloads = append(payloads, got...)` runs before the error is tested, but
`exchanged == 0 && lastErr != nil` returns `messageID, **nil**, lastErr`, and
`discoverOnInterface:222-224` turns any error into no devices.

This is precisely the asymmetry the comment eight lines below says was a bug for the
context case — "Reporting it as an error too made the caller discard that work" —
reintroduced for the socket-error case of the same shape.

**Fix:** `if exchanged == 0 && lastErr != nil && len(payloads) == 0`.

### B3. Any read error retires a listener's family permanently and silently
`wsd/listen.go:158-161` · *runtime-safety*

The loop cannot distinguish `net.ErrClosed` (cancellation) from a transient error, so one
error on the IPv4 socket returns that reader, closes its socket and drops its group
membership, while the IPv6 reader keeps the `WaitGroup` up and `out` open. The caller holds
a live channel that will never carry another IPv4 Hello and has no way to learn it: `Listen`
reports an error once, at open time.

### B4. The two IP families are probed in sequence
`wsd/discover.go:391` · *main session and runtime-safety, independently; measured*

`Timeout` is documented as the window of one exchange, and one exchange runs per family, in
series. Measured on a dual-stack interface: `Timeout: 2s` → **4.94 s elapsed**. At
`MaxProbeTimeout` that is ~181 s against a documented 90 s bound.

Worse, with the idiom a caller reaches for — `ctx, _ := context.WithTimeout(parent, Timeout)`
— the IPv4 exchange consumes the whole budget and the IPv6 exchange returns at its
`ctx.Err()` check (`wsd/discover.go:391`) having opened and thrown away a socket. **No error surfaces**,
because `exchanged == 1`. Measured: 2.00 s elapsed, IPv6 never got a collection window.

Meanwhile `bin/wsdc` fans out across *interfaces* concurrently (`bin/wsdc/discover.go:131`).
The library serialises the cheaper axis.

### B5. `Attempts` and `HopLimit` are unbounded while `Timeout` is clamped
`wsd/discover.go:183-209` · *main session, go-architect and untrusted-input, independently*

`wsd/doc.go:71` states the asymmetry as settled fact. `MaxProbeTimeout` exists for the
symmetrical reason given at `wsd/discover.go:91-110`: a value typed once or read from a config
file. `Attempts: 100000` holds the socket for hours *and* multicasts 100 000 datagrams at
the link — strictly worse than the case the ceiling was added for, because it also floods.

`HopLimit` is worse than reported. Measured: `SetMulticastTTL(256)` →
`setsockopt: invalid argument`. Traced through `setupV4` → `dialIPv4` → `exchange` →
`probe`, a `HopLimit` above 255 fails **every** family, so `Discover` returns
`setsockopt: invalid argument` for the whole interface — an opaque message for "your hop
limit is too large". A large-but-legal value pushes the Probe off the link that
`DefaultHopLimit = 1` exists to confine it to.

### B6. The truncation guard cannot fire
`wsd/discover.go:26,435`, `wsd/listen.go:162` · *main session and untrusted-input, same numbers*

`bufSize` is 65536. Measured on both families: the kernel refuses to send more than **65507**
(IPv4) or **65527** (IPv6) — `write: message too long` — and a maximum-size datagram reads
back at exactly those sizes. `n == len(buf)` is unreachable by 9 bytes.

So `TestReadRepliesDropsTruncated` and `TestReadRepliesReadsOnPastDroppedOversizeDatagrams`
characterise a situation no host on the link can produce, and the `MaxProbeTimeout` memo at
`wsd/discover.go:91-110` justifies the bound with that unreachable path — the real reason is A1.

Not exploitable. It is a false invariant that will mislead the next change: lowering
`bufSize` to 8 KiB would silently *discard* every legitimate reply above it rather than
truncate one.

### B7. `relatesTo` switches correlation off for an empty wanted ID
`wsd/parse.go:100-102` · *main session and untrusted-input, independently*

Every datagram on the link is accepted as a reply to us. Unreachable today — the only caller
passes `newMessageID()` — but it is the trapdoor a new entry point falls through, and
`SendProbe` deliberately does not return the identifier, so a helper parsing its output has
nothing but `""` to pass.

### B8. `Scopes` is emitted with no `MatchBy`, under a constant named for a rule never sent
`wsd/ws-discovery.go:107-109`, `wsd/flavor.go:26-28,47` · *wsd-protocol, sharpened here*

The April 2005 draft's own default when `@MatchBy` is omitted is **rfc2396**. The value
stored in `matchByRFC3986` is ONVIF Core §7.3.3's *override* of that default, presented by
the comment as WS-Discovery fact for both dialects.

The reviewer called the field merely dead. It is worse: `ProbeOptions.Scopes` is exported,
documented in the README table, and emitted at `wsd/ws-discovery.go:107-109` — and `MatchBy` appears nowhere in
the tree except that comment. So a caller who sets `Scopes` gets rfc2396 prefix matching,
while ONVIF devices are only required to support rfc3986. The code holds the right URI and
never sends it.

### B9. The CLI flags have no seam and are never asserted to reach `ProbeOptions`
`bin/wsdc/discover.go:87`, `bin/wsdc/listen.go:57` · *main session and cli-ux, independently*

`newDiscoverCmd`/`newListenCmd` hardcode `wsd.Discover`, `wsd.Listen` and `net.Interfaces`
inside their `RunE` closures, although `discover()`/`listen()` themselves take injectable
seams. Nothing asserts `--oasis11` sets `Flavor`, `--all` sets `IncludeNonOnvif`, or
`--types` reaches `PortTypes`. Invert either boolean and the whole suite still passes.

### B10. `SendProbe`'s godoc names two fields that do not exist
`wsd/discover.go:326` · *go-architect*

"The Types, Scopes and TypeNamespaces of opts shape the Probe." `ProbeOptions` has
`PortTypes` and `Scopes`; `Types` and `TypeNamespaces` are leftovers of the API `TypeName`
replaced. This is `godoc`-visible and is the one doc a caller of the raw path reads.

### B11. The announcement source is not unmapped
`wsd/listen.go:171` · *untrusted-input*

`readAnnouncements` assigns `ReadFromUDPAddrPort`'s address straight to `Device.From`, while
the probe path runs `Addr().Unmap()` (`wsd/discover.go:134-143`). Latent today; a caller
comparing `From` across the two paths, or against an allowlist, would compare
`10.0.0.1:3702` with `[::ffff:10.0.0.1]:3702` the day it changes.

### B12. `wsd/transport` is at 21.1% coverage; every 0% function is the socket layer
*main session and go-architect, independently*

`Dial`, `ListenMulticast`, `dialIPv4/v6`, `setupV4/V6`, `restrictToJoinedGroups` and all four
`v4Conn`/`v6Conn` adapters are uncovered. The adapters drop a control message and reorder
return values — the shape where a silent transposition lives — and `sourceOf`
(`wsd/discover.go:134-143`) type-asserts `*net.UDPAddr` on what they return, so an adapter
handing back the wrong `net.Addr` would zero every `Device.From` with no test failing. No
multicast is needed to cover them; a unicast loopback socket suffices.

## D. Decisions questioned, for the owner's call

### D1. Does `gosoap` earn its place?
`wsd` calls five methods of roughly twenty exported; all of `gosoap/ws-security.go` is unreachable
from `wsd`, since WS-Discovery carries no credentials.

**A reviewer's dependency argument here was wrong and is corrected below.** The honest cost
is 440 lines and an incoherent exported surface, not build weight.

Within the dead half: `Security` is exported with `Auth wsAuth`, an **exported field of an
unexported type**, so no external caller can write a `Security` literal; `newNonce` panics on
`rand.Read` failure (`gosoap/ws-security.go:90`); `generateToken` takes a `Username` it never
hashes (`gosoap/ws-security.go:124`); `AddWSSecurityAt` emits no `mustUnderstand` while the sample kept at
`gosoap/ws-security.go:51-60` shows `s:mustUnderstand="1"`. And `buildSoapRoot` puts an unused
`xmlns:soap-enc` on every Probe (`gosoap/soap-builder.go:242`).

### D2. `Discover` drops the port types; `Listen` keeps them.
`discoverOnInterface` reads `found.Types` to decide `isOnvifDevice` and discards it; `Device`
has no `Types` field while `Announcement` does. A caller who sets `IncludeNonOnvif` to see
non-ONVIF equipment cannot learn what any of it claimed to be.

### D3. The well-known types are mutable package variables.
`wsd/types.go:49-66`. Any code in the process can assign `wsd.TypeONVIFDevice = TypeName{}`
and silently empty every Probe built afterwards.

### D4. `SendProbe` is the only export whose own doc tells the reader to prefer another function,
and it returns uncorrelated unauthenticated payloads. go-architect would delete it.

### D5. The deadline-before-watchdog ordering is load-bearing and unenforceable.
`wsd/discover.go:437-457`. Inverting the two statements keeps the suite green at `-count=5`,
because `go func` almost never runs before the next statement — the historical bug is a real
but rare interleaving no test can reliably catch. The structural answer is
`context.AfterFunc(ctx, ...)` with `defer stop()` immediately below the deadline install,
which deletes the channel, the defer and the goroutine and makes the ordering visible. Same
substitution suits `wsd/listen.go:108-116`.

### D6. No `--attempts` or `--hop-limit` flag, though `--timeout` earned one.
An inconsistency, not a defect.

### D7. `listenWith` shares one unbuffered channel across both family readers,
so a slow consumer stalls the other family and its datagrams are dropped in the kernel
buffer. One slot per reader would decouple them.

## E. Verified sound

Recorded so the next audit need not re-derive it.

- **Parse-and-drop holds.** 5,001,481 fuzz executions over `parseProbeMatches` (with and
  without a wanted ID) and `parseAnnouncement`: no panic, no hang, no timeout.
  `documentRoot` is the only parsing entry point and both callers test its result.
- **No entity or DTD attack.** etree v1.7.1 leaves `ReadSettings.Entity` nil and sets
  `Strict`, so a billion-laughs DTD fails to parse; nesting is capped at etree's depth limit
  of 1024.
- **The wire bytes are correct.** All four dialect URIs per flavor, the SOAP-over-UDP §4
  retransmission constants and algorithm, hop limit 1, port 3702, both multicast groups, the
  SOAP 1.2 envelope namespace and `mustUnderstand`'s envelope-namespace qualification, and
  `urn:uuid:` — each checked against the primary specification text, not against the comment.
- **`parseXAddrs` is the filter it claims to be**: host required, http/https only, no
  userinfo; `url.Parse` independently rejects ASCII control bytes.
- **Exact namespace matching in `isOnvifDevice`** is correct, and the `nsScopes`
  memoisation has not regressed: the crafted 74 KB datagram parses in 4.7 ms, linear.
- **The terminal boundary is complete.** Every `fmt.Fprint*` in `bin/wsdc` carrying link- or
  argv-derived text goes through `orDash` or `graphicOnly`.
- **`listenWith` close ordering** is right: defers unwind `close(stop)` → `conn.Close()` →
  `wg.Done()`, so the watchdog retires before its socket goes; no double close, no send on
  closed. The double `conn.Close()` on `*net.UDPConn` is safe.
- **The `PacketConn` contract** at `wsd/transport/transport.go:24-28` holds against
  x/net v0.58.0: `SetReadDeadline` concurrent with or after `Close` delegates to
  `net.UDPConn` and is race-free.
- **Context propagation** is honoured on every path below `main`; no
  `context.Background()`/`TODO()`.
- **These tests do pin what they claim**: `TestListenStopsWhenTheCallerAbandonsTheChannel`
  (goroutine-count based), `TestListenClosesEverySocketOnCancel`,
  `TestDiscoverProbesEveryInterfaceAtOnce` (barrier, not stopwatch),
  `TestREADMEDocumentsProbeOptionDefaults`, `TestREADMEListsEveryFlagTheTreeRegisters`,
  `TestLicenceHeaderOnEveryGoFile`, `TestLibrariesImportNoLogger`.
- **The seams are the right shape.** `prober`, `opener` and the three `bin/wsdc` func types
  are argument-passed, not package variables, for the reason `bin/wsdc/interfaces.go:17-29`
  gives.
